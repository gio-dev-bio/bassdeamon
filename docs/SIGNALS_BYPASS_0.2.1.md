## Signal coverage

717 DSP cases / 243,025,200 aggregate L/R output samples. Signals: impulses and adjacent/block-boundary impulses; 20 Hz–18 kHz sweep; 47/113/997/10003 Hz multitone; deterministic white and low-pass noise; saw bass; kick/bass mixture; −90 dBFS sine; subnormals; signed zeros; near-full-scale sine; DC; antiphase and independent stereo.

44.1/48/96 kHz; buffers 1/17/64/127/512/2048 for initial module-off tests. Active render tests use buffer 127, 4 seconds with input stopped at 2 seconds. GLUE/UNMASK/PHASE/all, extreme width/depth/attack/release/amount automation, active-to-off, and neutral controls are exercised. Fixed-parameter rendering with 64-sample blocks matches variable blocks of 1–511 samples exactly.

- Zero NaN/Inf.
- Numerical identity in all module-off, GLUE amount zero, UNMASK amount zero, neutral PHASE, UNMASK without kick, and sidechain-only DELTA tests.
- Silent right channel remains zero when only left carries signal, at all three sample rates.
- Antiphase stereo retains exact opposite polarity in the checked module renders.
- Largest output peak 0.983878 FS (approximately −0.14 dBFS); no clipping in these fixtures/settings. This is not a limiter guarantee.
- Largest filter residue after 1 second of silence: 1.514e−22 FS, below −120 dBFS.

"Numerically identical" is not always bitwise IEEE identity in the processing core: −0 can become +0. That distinction is preserved in the raw results.

## Host bypass

504 processor cases, 1,046,682 compared samples: **zero differing bits**, including signed zeros and subnormals. Mono/stereo, mono sidechain, three sample rates and six buffer sizes. PHASE inversion/delay/rotation, AGC, output +6 dB are set before bypass. Declared latency remains zero.

The test calls the actual JUCE `processBlockBypassed`; this is the path used by the local JUCE 9.0.5 VST3 wrapper for its generated bypass parameter. It is not an end-to-end Cubase/hardware measurement.

## Reproduced switching discontinuity

With an inverted 80 Hz sine (−12.04 dBFS peak), switching at a nonzero sample to hard host bypass produces a −0.243164 FS sample jump. Sixth-order Butterworth high-pass >2 kHz peak around the switch: −16.77 dBFS. This can produce a click: immediate bypass and continuity across different waveform polarities are separate properties.

The GUI global ON switch uses module smoothing with a 20 ms time constant. In the same example its HF peak is −74.17 dBFS. It reduces the sudden discontinuity but fades through a different waveform/amplitude; it is not an instantaneous bitwise copy.

The global switch also flushes subnormals in JUCE's denormal-protected processing path and may change −0 to +0. With a preceding active multitone/phase/delay/AGC/output-trim state, the largest remaining error after 500 ms is 1.644e−13 FS (about −256 dBFS). Therefore do not label the GUI ON switch as an immediate bit-perfect hard bypass.

## Reproduce and limits

Run `signal_tests results`, `plugin_tests --signals results`, then `python benchmarks/analyze_signals.py results output`. Raw CSVs and summary are under `benchmarks/results/signals-2026-11-08/`. The macOS run validates the processor natively on ARM; the dependency-free DSP suite also ran on Linux x86_64. These tests are complementary to earlier native Intel/ARM benchmarks and pluginval validation, and are not proof of universal absence of aliasing/IMD or artifacts on every signal/parameter setting.
